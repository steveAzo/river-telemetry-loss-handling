\section{How the Records Lose Data}
\label{sec:outages}

In the water-quality deployments, most missing data were in long gaps. InWaterSense node~1 had \inwsOutages{} delivery gaps (timelines in Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:s-records}). Of these, \inwsOneSlotEvents{} lasted a single slot but held only \inwsOneSlotLost{} of the missing readings, while \inwsDayEvents{} gaps of a day or more held \inwsDayLost{} (95\% CI \inwsDayLostLo--\inwsDayLostHi), and gaps of 6~h or more held \inwsSixLost{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:outage-share}); the longest lasted \inwsMaxOutageDays~days. Weija, on another continent, showed the same structure: \weijaCondDayLost{} of the missing conductivity readings fell in gaps of a day or more (\weijaDayLostMin--\weijaDayLostMax{} across all streams), with the longest lasting \weijaMaxOutageDays~days. With only \weijaCondDayN{} day-long gaps, the Weija interval is wide (\weijaCondDayLostLo--\weijaCondDayLostHi); therefore, Weija serves as qualitative support.

The geometric model failed, and no tested model fitted. The geometric model (\ref{eq:geo}) ranked last by AIC on every record (Table~\ref{tab:aic}), by \aicGeomGapInws{} points on InWaterSense, \aicGeomGapWeija{} on Weija, and \aicGeomGapUva{} on UVA. It also failed in the quantity that matters: fitted to InWaterSense, it put \geomSixInws{} of the missing readings in gaps of 6~h or more, against \inwsSixLost{} observed, and \geomDayInws{} in gaps of a day or more. It was also rejected absolutely on every record ($p\le$\,\gofPmin). On the large records, every model was rejected, but the geometric model's largest distance between the fitted and observed distribution functions was \gofGeomDInws{} on InWaterSense, against \gofBestDInws{} for the best alternative. No single alternative won everywhere; the mixture (\ref{eq:mix}), with many short losses and a few long gaps, is the common thread. On InWaterSense, long gaps also clustered (Spearman $\rho=$\,\inwsRhoNextOut{} between the lengths of consecutive gaps); therefore, all simulations replay the real records rather than a fitted model.

The LoRaWAN networks showed short radio losses and rare system outages. On LoED, well-heard devices lost \loedRadioLoss{} of their frames to the radio, in short bursts: of \loedBursts{} bursts, \loedBurstOne{} were a single frame and \loedBurstFive{} were at most five frames, and a loss after a loss had a probability of \loedCondLoss. UVA adds 16 months of continuous operation. The system was available \uvaAvail{} of the time; of the \uvaMissing{} missing frames, \uvaRadioShare{} were radio losses and \uvaSysShare{} were system losses, and \uvaSysDayShare{} of the system downtime fell in outages of a day or more. One gateway was down for \uvaGwCDays{} days without causing a single gateway loss, because every sensor was also heard by a second gateway, and rain had no detectable effect on link loss (\uvaRainDry{} dry, \uvaRainWet{} rainy; Wilcoxon~\cite{wilcoxon1945individual} $p=$\,\uvaRainP).

In response to RQ1, in the four records, losses fell into short, nearly independent radio losses and rarer long delivery gaps or system outages. In the water-quality deployments, gaps of 6~h or more held most of the missing data (\inwsSixLost{} on InWaterSense, \weijaCondSixLost{} on Weija). A geometric gap-length model missed this second regime almost entirely.
