\section{Data and Models}
\label{sec:data}

\emph{Terms.} A \emph{reading} is one sampling cycle of a node: the four parameters taken at one instant, which travel together. Time is divided into \emph{slots} at the reporting cadence, one reading per slot. A \emph{delivery gap} is a run of slots for which no reading reached the server, whatever the cause; a \emph{system outage} is a delivery gap verified to affect every node of a network (its gateway, backhaul or server); a \emph{radio loss} is a frame lost while the rest of the path worked.

\subsection{Delivery records and the cause of gaps}
\label{sec:data-records}
The study uses four records of what a network delivered (Table~\ref{tab:records}; details in Supplement Sec.~\ref{sec:s-records}). \emph{InWaterSense} (Kosovo): node~1 on the Sitnica river~\cite{ahmedi2022dataset,ahmedi2021inwatersense}, reporting about every 611~s from May~2015 to January~2016. \emph{Weija} (Ghana): \weijaStreams{} parameter streams of one node, October~2018 to March~2019~\cite{adumanu2020smart}; records marked as imputed do not count, and gap statistics use the conductivity stream (\weijaCondStepMin-min cadence). \emph{UVA} (USA): ten LoRaWAN sensors reporting every 5~min through three gateways, February~2024 to June~2025~\cite{nikseresht2025uva,nikseresht2025uvadata}; missing frames are found from frame counters and classed as \emph{system} losses if no sensor reached the database in that 10-min bin, as gateway losses if all of the sensor's gateways were silent, and as radio losses otherwise. \emph{LoED} (UK): uplinks heard by nine gateways~\cite{bhatia2020loed,bhatia2020loeddata}, of which only well-heard devices (at least 200 frames, radio loss below 20\%) are used.

Neither water-quality record has a node status log, so a gap in which the network was down cannot be told apart directly from one in which the node produced nothing. Two partial checks are possible. On Weija, the node's other streams share its logger and uplink: of the \weijaEcMissing{} missing conductivity slots, \weijaNodeSilentShare{} were silences of the whole node, and in gaps of 6~h or more only \weijaLongAliveShare{} of the slots had another stream delivering. The long gaps are node-wide silences (node, logger or uplink). InWaterSense's two nodes reached the server through one gateway and one GPRS link~\cite{ahmedi2022dataset}; while node~2 was operating, it was silent in \inwsNodeTwoSilent{} of node~1's missing slots, and after correcting for its own silence, gaps of 6~h or more were about \inwsSharedLong{} shared-cause (gateway, backhaul, server or shared power) and \inwsSpecificLong{} specific to node~1. Most long gaps are therefore failures of the path to the server, but some may be nodes that produced nothing; Sec.~\ref{sec:handling} treats buffering's recovery as an upper bound.

\subsection{Gap-length models}
\label{sec:data-outages}
A record is reduced to alternating up-runs and gaps of $k_1,\dots,k_n$ slots; let $h(k)$ be the duration of a gap of $k$ slots. Because loss handling depends on where the missing data are, the main statistic is the share of missing slots in gaps of at least duration $L$,
\begin{equation}
S(L)=\frac{\sum_{i:\,h(k_i)\ge L} k_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} k_i},
\label{eq:share}
\end{equation}
with bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals~\cite{efron1979bootstrap}. The gap lengths are fitted by maximum likelihood with a geometric model,
\begin{equation}
P_\mathrm{geo}(k)=(1-q)\,q^{k-1},
\label{eq:geo}
\end{equation}
the gap-length distribution of any loss process with a single loss state, including independent losses and the Gilbert--Elliott channel; a two-geometric mixture,
\begin{equation}
P_\mathrm{mix}(k)=w\,(1-q_1)\,q_1^{k-1}+(1-w)\,(1-q_2)\,q_2^{k-1},
\label{eq:mix}
\end{equation}
a Markov chain with one up state and two loss states (short loss, long gap); a discrete lognormal; and a discrete power law. Models are ranked by Akaike's information criterion and tested absolutely by a bootstrap Kolmogorov--Smirnov test (Supplement Sec.~\ref{sec:s-models}).

\subsection{Energy model and loss-handling strategies}
\label{sec:data-energy}
The node is a Waspmote-class host~\cite{libelium2020plugsense} with NB-IoT (the N211 module with release assistance~\cite{michelinakis2021dissecting}), LoRaWAN SF7 or LoRaWAN SF12 (EU868). Each transmission attempt is charged the measured energy of its radio and outcome, host time included (Supplement Sec.~\ref{sec:s-energy}). NB-IoT energies come from UDP datagrams to an echo server~\cite{michelinakis2021dissecting}, so a confirmed report is acknowledged by the application, end to end; a cell search without a network costs the receive power~\cite{sorensen2022nbiot} times a search time swept over 2.2--60~s. LoRaWAN energies come from state-by-state current profiles~\cite{casals2017lorawan}: at SF7 the acknowledgement arrives in the first receive window, so a confirmed success is cheaper than an unconfirmed report; at SF12 it is dearer. The acknowledgement comes from the network server. LoRaWAN 1.0.2 recommends eight transmissions of a confirmed frame with the data rate lowered every two~\cite{lora2016spec102}; LoRaWAN 1.0.4 sends a frame NbTrans times (1--15) at a fixed rate~\cite{lora2020spec104}; both are evaluated. The 1\% duty cycle spaces frames~\cite{lora2021rp002}. A bulk frame carries \rpfNb{} readings on NB-IoT, \rpfSfSeven{} at SF7 and \rpfSfTwelve{} at SF12. Over a record of $D$ days, the transmission energy per day and the lifetime on a battery of energy $B$ are
\begin{equation}
E_\mathrm{tx}=\frac{1}{D}\sum_{a} e_a,\qquad Y=\frac{B}{365\,(E_\mathrm{floor}+E_\mathrm{tx})},
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
where $e_a$ is the energy of attempt $a$ and $E_\mathrm{floor}$ the daily sleep and sensing energy. Absolute energy depends on the platform and stack: an independent measurement on another board~\cite{ugwuanyi2021developing} reports \ugwLoraRatio{} this model's per-message energy on LoRaWAN and \ugwNbRatio{} on NB-IoT (TCP without release assistance). The results are therefore ratios between strategies on the same radio; lifetimes are illustrative.

The strategies are: \emph{unconfirmed} (one attempt per reading); \emph{unconfirmed + back-off} (NB-IoT; the fair baseline, because a node that searches for a cell in every slot of a gap wastes energy); \emph{ARQ} (up to 8 transmissions on LoRaWAN, 3 on NB-IoT); \emph{ARQ + buffer}, in which a failed reading is buffered and the node sends one small confirmed probe at a time, waiting
\begin{equation}
w_n=\min\left(2^{n},\,K\right)\ \text{slots},\qquad K=6~\text{h},
\label{eq:backoff}
\end{equation}
after the $n$-th failed probe and uploading the buffer in confirmed bulk frames once a probe succeeds; and \emph{batching}, which uploads its buffer every 1 or 6~h with the same back-off.

\emph{Replay.} A slot in which a record delivered nothing is a slot in which the first attempt fails; what happens to later attempts in that slot is not in the record. Three models bracket it: the link stays down for the whole slot (full slot, pessimistic for ARQ); it returns at a uniformly random time before the next delivered slot (restoration); or, in addition, gaps of up to an hour are radio-loss bursts through which retries usually pass (burst, optimistic for ARQ). Independent radio losses of 0.096 per attempt (LoED) can be added. The buffer is unlimited and free unless stated. Supplement Table~\ref{tab:s-assumptions} lists every input as measured, inferred or assumed.

\subsection{The server's real-time picture and the tolerances}
\label{sec:data-picture}
Let $x(t)$ be the true reading of one parameter at slot $t$, $A(\tau)$ the set of slots whose readings have arrived by time $\tau$, and $s^\ast(\tau)=\max A(\tau)$. The server shows
\begin{equation}
\hat{x}(\tau)=\begin{cases}
x(\tau), & \tau\in A(\tau),\\
x(s^\ast)+\Delta(\tau), & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:picture}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta(\tau)=x(\tau-24\,\mathrm{h})-x(s^\ast-24\,\mathrm{h})$ if both readings have arrived and $\Delta(\tau)=0$ otherwise: the latest reading plus the site's own change over the same hours a day earlier (the \emph{own-diel} estimator). The picture is scored as the share of slots with $|\hat{x}(\tau)-x(\tau)|\le\delta$. At the reference level L2, $\delta$ is 0.2~$^\circ$C (T), 2\%~saturation (DO), 0.1 (pH) and 2\% of the median (EC); L1 halves and L3 widens these. L2 lies at or inside the U.S. Geological Survey's accuracy rating ``excellent''~\cite{wagner2006usgs} for every parameter (Supplement Sec.~\ref{sec:s-design}).

\emph{River data.} River signals are approved USGS instantaneous values of T, EC, DO and pH~\cite{usgs2023nwis}, with DO converted to percent saturation~\cite{benson1984oxygen}. Sec.~\ref{sec:reconstruct} uses \chainCount{} river chains (\chainSites{} sites) in 2023; Sec.~\ref{sec:design} uses every candidate site with all four parameters in complete cycles for at least half of the year: \designSites{} of \designCandidates{} in 2023 (development) and \confSites{} in 2024 (held out).
