\section{Can Reconstruction Replace Delivery?}
\label{sec:reconstruct}

If a server could estimate missing readings sufficiently well, it would not need them delivered. This was tested on \chainCountWord{} river chains, i.e., sets of USGS sites along the same river (\chainSites{} sites), with each pair of sites serving as target and neighbour in both orders. On a 1-h grid, gaps of \chainGaps~h were simulated at the target in the last 80\% of 2023. Two families of estimators were compared:
\begin{itemize}
\item Real-time estimators use data up to the gap and the neighbour during it: persistence; the own-diel estimator of (\ref{eq:picture}); the neighbour's scaled change (lag and slope fitted on the first 20\% of the year); and own diel plus the part of the neighbour's change that its own diel pattern does not explain.
\item Bridging estimators also know the value after the gap, as after a backfill: linear interpolation, and interpolation plus the neighbour's departure from its own line.
\end{itemize}
The equations are given in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:s-reconstruct}. The error on a gap is its maximum absolute error; confidence intervals resample whole chains, and pairs within them.

In real time, a site's own diel pattern was the estimator to beat (Table~\ref{tab:chains}). For day-long gaps, it beat persistence in \chainDielBeatsPersistDo{} of pairs for DO, and a neighbour improved on the better own-data estimator in only \chainRtWinDo{} of pairs for DO, \chainRtWinPh{} for pH, and \chainRtWinEc{} for EC; temperature, with its shared weather driver, was the exception (\chainRtWinT). When bridging, the neighbour helped T (\chainBfWinT{} of pairs) and DO (\chainBfWinDo), but not pH or EC.

A naive baseline overestimates the value of neighbours. Against persistence alone, a neighbour won in \chainNaiveWinDo{} of DO pairs, cutting the median error from \chainPersistDo{} to \chainBestNbDo~\%sat; however, the own-diel estimator already reached \chainDielDo~\%sat. Much of what a naive test credits to spatial correlation is the shared day--night cycle. The fitted relation also did not follow the flow: a downstream neighbour got a positive lag approximately as often as an upstream one (\lagPosDown{} vs \lagPosUp{} for DO); therefore, the lag tracks shared timing rather than travel time. Evaluations of spatio-temporal reconstruction on rivers therefore need a per-site diel baseline and spatial structure from the river network~\cite{peterson2010mixed,jing2024casper}.

No estimator kept day-long gaps reliably within tolerance. For 1-h gaps, the better own-data estimator kept \chainTolOneH{} of gaps within the L2 tolerance; for 24-h gaps, the best of all six kept \recDayAll{} (95\% CI \recDayAllLo--\recDayAllHi), from \recDayT{} for T to \recDayPh{} for pH.

\subsection{Contemporary deep imputers}
\label{sec:rec-deep}
To test whether this is a limit of reconstruction or of simple estimators, SAITS, a self-attention imputer~\cite{du2023saits}, and StemGNN, a graph neural network~\cite{cao2020stemgnn}, were trained per chain on the first half of 2023, with whole-site gaps of 1--72~h among their training masks. They were tested on whole-site gaps in the second half, on the same \deepGaps{} cases as the simple estimators (protocol in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:s-reconstruct}). They kept fewer gaps within tolerance: \deepBridgeOneSaits{} (SAITS) and \deepBridgeOneStem{} (StemGNN) of 1-h gaps when bridging, against \deepBridgeOneInterp{} for interpolation, and \deepBridgeDaySaits{} and \deepBridgeDayStem{} of day-long gaps, against \deepBridgeDayInterp. In real time, they kept \deepRtDaySaits{} and \deepRtDayStem{} of day-long gaps, against \deepRtDayDiel{} for own diel. They helped in one place: when bridging a day-long gap, the median error of SAITS was \deepDayDoSaits~\%sat for DO and \deepDayPhSaits{} for pH, against \deepDayDoInterp{} and \deepDayPhInterp{} for interpolation. No estimator, simple or deep, kept more than \deepDayBestAny{} of the day-long gaps of any parameter within tolerance.

In response to RQ3, the evaluated estimators, simple or deep, cannot replace delivery for the day-long gaps that hold most of the missing data. Reconstruction plays the role of the interim estimate between deliveries, which is the role it plays in the design of Section~\ref{sec:design}.
