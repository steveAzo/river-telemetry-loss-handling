\section{Can Reconstruction Replace Delivery?}
\label{sec:reconstruct}

If a server could estimate missing readings sufficiently well, it would not need them delivered. This was tested on \chainCountWord{} river chains, i.e., sets of USGS sites along the same river (\chainSites{} sites), with each pair of sites serving as target and neighbour in both orders. On a 1-h grid, gaps of \chainGaps~h were simulated at the target in the last 80\% of 2023 (a median of \chainOutages{} per pair, parameter, and length). A gap covers the slots after the last observed slot $t_0$ and before the next observed slot $t_1$. Let $y$ be the neighbour's series, lagged by $\ell$ hours, and $b$ a scaling slope; the lag $\ell\in[0,48]$~h maximises the correlation between 6-h changes of the target and the lagged neighbour in the first 20\% of the year, and $b$ is the least-squares slope of those changes (pairs whose best correlation is negative are dropped). Two families of estimators were compared. Real-time estimators use only data up to $t_0$ and the neighbour during the gap:
\begin{align}
\hat{x}_\mathrm{pers}(t)&=x(t_0), \label{eq:pers}\\
\hat{x}_\mathrm{diel}(t)&=x(t_0)+x(t-24\,\mathrm{h})-x(t_0-24\,\mathrm{h}), \label{eq:diel}\\
\hat{x}_\mathrm{nb}(t)&=x(t_0)+b\,[y(t)-y(t_0)], \label{eq:nb}\\
\hat{x}_\mathrm{dn}(t)&=\hat{x}_\mathrm{diel}(t)+b\,[y(t)-y(t_0)]-b\,[y(t-24\,\mathrm{h})-y(t_0-24\,\mathrm{h})]. \label{eq:dn}
\end{align}
Persistence (\ref{eq:pers}) holds the last value; own diel (\ref{eq:diel}) is the server's estimator (\ref{eq:picture}); neighbour change (\ref{eq:nb}) adds the neighbour's scaled change since the gap began; and diel + neighbour (\ref{eq:dn}) adds only the part of that change that the neighbour's own diel pattern does not explain. Bridging estimators also know $x(t_1)$, as after a backfill:
\begin{align}
\hat{x}_\mathrm{int}(t)&=x(t_0)+\frac{t-t_0}{t_1-t_0}\,[x(t_1)-x(t_0)], \label{eq:int}\\
\hat{x}_\mathrm{in}(t)&=\hat{x}_\mathrm{int}(t)+b\,[y(t)-\bar{y}(t)], \label{eq:in}
\end{align}
where $\bar{y}$ is the straight line from $y(t_0)$ to $y(t_1)$; linear interpolation (\ref{eq:int}) is the first bridging estimator, and (\ref{eq:in}) adds the neighbour's departure from its own line. The error on a gap is its maximum absolute error; confidence intervals resample whole chains, and pairs within them. Relative gains exclude the pH pairs ($n=\chainGainExcluded$) whose target (site \chainExclSite) has a median error of exactly zero. The neighbours lie \pairKmMin{} to \pairKmMax~km from their targets (median \pairKmMed~km), with the order along the river taken from the drainage area in the USGS site record.

In real time, a site's own diel pattern was the estimator to beat (Table~\ref{tab:chains}). For day-long gaps, it beat persistence in \chainDielBeatsPersistDo{} of pairs for DO, and a neighbour improved on the better own-data estimator in only \chainRtWinDo{} of pairs for DO, \chainRtWinPh{} for pH, and \chainRtWinEc{} for EC; temperature, with its shared weather driver, was the exception (\chainRtWinT). When bridging, the neighbour helped T (\chainBfWinT{} of pairs) and DO (\chainBfWinDo), but not pH or EC.

A naive baseline overestimates the value of neighbours. Against persistence alone, a neighbour won in \chainNaiveWinDo{} of DO pairs, cutting the median error from \chainPersistDo{} to \chainBestNbDo~\%sat; however, the own-diel estimator already reached \chainDielDo~\%sat. Much of what a naive test credits to spatial correlation is the shared day--night cycle. The fitted relation also did not follow the flow: a downstream neighbour had a positive lag approximately as often as an upstream one (\lagPosDown{} vs \lagPosUp{} for DO); therefore, the lag tracks shared timing rather than travel time. Evaluations of spatio-temporal reconstruction on rivers therefore need a per-site diel baseline and spatial structure from the river network~\cite{peterson2010mixed,jing2024casper}.

No estimator kept day-long gaps reliably within tolerance. For 1-h gaps, the better own-data estimator kept \chainTolOneH{} of gaps within the L2 tolerance; for 24-h gaps, the best of all six kept \recDayAll{} (95\% CI \recDayAllLo--\recDayAllHi), from \recDayT{} for T to \recDayPh{} for pH.

\subsection{Contemporary deep imputers}
\label{sec:rec-deep}
To test whether this is a limit of reconstruction or of simple estimators, SAITS, a self-attention imputer~\cite{du2023saits}, and StemGNN, a graph neural network~\cite{cao2020stemgnn} (PyPOTS implementations), see all four parameters of every site of a chain in 192-h windows, normalised per window and series from the observed values. They were trained per chain on the first half of 2023, with the library's random point masks and, in half of the windows, a whole site masked for 1--72~h; the checkpoint with the best error on whole-site blocks in the last 15\% of that half was kept. StemGNN used one spectral block, one stack, and width 16 (7.5~million parameters). A first configuration, normalised with half-year means and spreads, under-fitted by an order of magnitude even on 1-h gaps and was discarded before any comparison. The models were tested on whole-site gaps of 1, 6, 24, and 72~h in the second half, the same \deepGaps{} cases on which the simple estimators were scored, with the neighbour chosen and fitted on the first half. GRIN and SPIN need a graph library that the available machine could not hold, and ImputeFormer did not finish training in the available time. The deep imputers kept fewer gaps within tolerance: \deepBridgeOneSaits{} (SAITS) and \deepBridgeOneStem{} (StemGNN) of 1-h gaps when bridging, against \deepBridgeOneInterp{} for interpolation, and \deepBridgeDaySaits{} and \deepBridgeDayStem{} of day-long gaps, against \deepBridgeDayInterp. In real time, they kept \deepRtDaySaits{} and \deepRtDayStem{} of day-long gaps, against \deepRtDayDiel{} for own diel. The one exception was bridging a day-long gap, for which SAITS had the lower median error: \deepDayDoSaits~\%sat for DO and \deepDayPhSaits{} for pH, against \deepDayDoInterp{} and \deepDayPhInterp{} for interpolation. No estimator, simple or deep, kept more than \deepDayBestAny{} of the day-long gaps of any parameter within tolerance.

In response to RQ3, the evaluated estimators, simple or deep, cannot replace delivery for the day-long gaps that hold most of the missing data. Reconstruction plays the role of the interim estimate between deliveries, which is the role it plays in the design of Section~\ref{sec:design}.
