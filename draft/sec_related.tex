\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{How sensor networks lose data}
Early measurements of dense wireless sensor networks showed that packet delivery varies widely between links and over time~\cite{zhao2003understanding}. Kong et al.~\cite{kong2014dataloss} analysed three real sensor datasets with losses of 23\%, 35\% and 64\%, and synthesised five named loss patterns, from random element loss to ``successive loss in a row'' when a node is damaged or out of energy; the patterns are motivated by their causes, but their durations are not measured. Deployment reports describe the long-gap regime anecdotally. In the CSIRO water-quality network, losses occurred at the gateway when its server connection was down, and end-to-end recovery from RAM copies improved delivery by at most 10.6\%~\cite{ledinh2007design}. A solar-powered gateway lost power every night until after sunrise~\cite{langendoen2006murphy}; a GPRS sink failed to reconnect after disconnections until it was rebooted remotely~\cite{ingelrest2010sensorscope}; a gateway's modem silently disappeared from its operating system until a daily watchdog reset was added~\cite{brzozowski2018lessons}; and a sub-Arctic LoRaWAN deployment saw outages of days to weeks from gateway, power and connectivity faults~\cite{puluckul2025subarctic}. For LoRaWAN, public datasets now allow loss to be measured from frame counters: LoED records the traffic of nine gateways in London~\cite{bhatia2020loed}, and a 16-month deployment at the University of Virginia (UVA) records ten sensors, three gateways and co-located weather~\cite{nikseresht2025uva}. No previous study appears to have measured gap durations across several such records, fitted models to them, and asked what they imply for the choice of loss-handling remedy.

\subsection{Estimation and retransmission under loss}
Kalman filtering with intermittent observations~\cite{sinopoli2004kalman} established that estimation error stays bounded only if observations arrive often enough, under independent (Bernoulli) arrivals. Work on real-time remote estimation asks whether a failed packet should be retransmitted or replaced by a fresh measurement, with independent or finite-state Markov fading channels~\cite{huang2019retransmit,huang2020harq}, and finds switching-type optimal policies. Age of information formalises timeliness~\cite{yates2021aoi}, and semantic-aware remote estimation schedules redundant sensors that observe the same source~\cite{sun2026overlapping}. These works supply the vocabulary of the question studied here, retransmit or refresh, but not its setting: multi-day gaps, battery-limited LPWAN radios, and environmental signals with strong daily cycles. On LoRaWAN, application-layer coding recovers frames lost to the radio from redundancy in later frames~\cite{marcelis2017dare}; it addresses radio loss, not gaps in which nothing gets through.

\subsection{Reconstruction of missing sensor data}
Compressive and low-rank data gathering exploit spatial and temporal correlation to recover readings that were not collected or were lost~\cite{luo2009cdg,cheng2013stcdg,piao2014lowrank,kong2014dataloss}. Deep spatio-temporal imputation is the current state of the art: graph neural networks such as GRIN~\cite{cini2022grin}, SPIN~\cite{marisca2022spin} and StemGNN~\cite{cao2020stemgnn}, self-attention models such as SAITS~\cite{du2023saits} and ImputeFormer~\cite{nie2024imputeformer}, and causality-aware graph models that discount spurious spatial correlation~\cite{jing2024casper}. GRIN and SPIN were evaluated mainly on random point losses and on simulated block failures of 12--48 steps (1--4~h at 5-minute steps), and deep compressed sensing for urban environmental monitoring on completely random losses~\cite{chang2023deepcs}. The simulated failures thus last hours, not days, and the GRIN and SPIN evaluations include no per-site diel (seasonal-naive) baseline, although several of their signals have strong day--night cycles. River networks have a specific spatial structure, along the flow, that geostatistical models represent explicitly~\cite{peterson2010mixed}. This paper tests upstream and downstream neighbours on the same river, and two of the deep imputers, against a site's own diel pattern, on gaps of up to three days.

\subsection{Store-and-forward in practice, and LPWAN energy}
Buffering on the node and forwarding later is established practice. The CSIRO team proposed flash buffering ``to handle network outage better''~\cite{ledinh2007design}; the potato-field deployment planned node-side logging with a daily network-wide acknowledgement~\cite{langendoen2006murphy}; SeNDT stored all lake water-quality data on the node for later collection from the shore~\cite{mcdonald2007sendt}; Arctic tundra nodes keep append-only logs and advance their upload position only after an acknowledgement~\cite{murphy2021arctic}; sub-Arctic nodes oversample and buffer in flash~\cite{puluckul2025subarctic}; and a disruption-tolerant gateway stores LoRaWAN messages while its cellular backhaul is down~\cite{zennaro2025kyrgyzstan}. The same reports document the pitfalls: draining a backlog can starve live traffic~\cite{ingelrest2010sensorscope}, and the buffer's storage can itself fail~\cite{murphy2021arctic,brzozowski2018lessons}. What is missing is a quantitative comparison of these remedies, on energy, completeness and timeliness, under measured gap processes. The energy model of this paper rests on published measurements: state-by-state LoRaWAN current profiles~\cite{casals2017lorawan}, NB-IoT energy per report with release assistance~\cite{michelinakis2021dissecting}, and NB-IoT power states~\cite{sorensen2022nbiot}. Analytical models describe the NB-IoT latency--energy trade-off~\cite{azari2019nbiot}, and side-by-side measurements compare LoRaWAN and NB-IoT energy in a developing-country setting~\cite{ugwuanyi2021developing}.

\subsection{Prediction-based reporting}
Dual prediction schemes run the same predictor at the node and the server and transmit only when the server's prediction would leave a tolerance~\cite{dias2016survey,santini2006adaptive}; recent work combines dual prediction with adaptive sampling under a hard energy budget~\cite{arnaiz2024adaptive}. These schemes are usually evaluated with reliable or independently lossy links, and their purpose is to suppress reports. This paper uses the same mechanism for a different purpose: to decide when an hourly batch must leave early because the server's estimate would otherwise leave tolerance, and it makes the mechanism model when data arrive, not only whether.
