\begin{abstract}
Battery-powered river water-quality telemetry suffers short radio losses and long delivery gaps, yet recovery strategies are usually evaluated under independent packet-loss assumptions. This study characterised losses in four operational records (two water-quality deployments and two LoRaWAN networks) and evaluated retransmission, buffering, and reconstruction with trace-driven energy models for LoRaWAN (long-range wide-area network) and narrowband Internet of Things (NB-IoT). Gaps of at least 24~h held \inwsDayLost{} and \weijaCondDayLost{} of the missing readings in the two water-quality records, and a geometric gap-length model was rejected (bootstrap test, $p \le \gofPmin$). Automatic repeat request (ARQ) recovered less than one-fifth of the missing readings, even under optimistic retry timing, at \arqRatioMin--\arqRatioMax{} the energy of unconfirmed LoRaWAN reporting, whereas buffering with back-off recovered all generated readings. Neither simple nor deep reconstruction kept most 24-h gaps within tolerance. An outage-aware scheme (hourly batching, immediate alarm uploads, a bounded-error trigger, and buffered backfill) was tested with frozen rules on held-out 2024 data from \confSites{} river sites. With the link up, it matched per-reading reporting in alarm latency for detected events and in the share of server estimates within tolerance (\hFiveDiffMinConf{} to \hFiveDiffMaxConf{} percentage points), while per-reading ARQ with buffering used \arqOverBoundedMinConf--\arqOverBoundedMaxConf{} the transmission energy and \nodeArqOverFinalMin--\nodeArqOverFinalMax{} the node energy. These findings support outage-aware buffering and selective reporting.
\end{abstract}
