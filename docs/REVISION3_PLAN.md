## What changes in the paper's identity

- Contribution type: measured evidence and design guidance for energy-efficient water-quality telemetry under real outages, not a new mechanism. Reviewers at the target venue must find findings that change practice, each with a number, a mechanism, and a held-out check.
- New evidence from the rescoping (docs/RESCOPING_2026-10-31.md, Section 7), all on development data, to be considered for inclusion:
  - Oracle bounds: a cross-validated optimal probing schedule saves 0.4-2.9% of the design's energy over capped exponential back-off on LoRaWAN and up to 9% on NB-IoT (InWaterSense, unmeasured search time) (G2); a clairvoyant joint policy saves 0-3% of total node energy on two records, 12-13% on the worst (S8).
  - Energy split: with hourly batching, sensing is 40-89% of node energy and the radio 5-45%.
  - Shared recovery: one steady state per cell size, no post-outage trap (S4).
  - Gateway diversity: in UVA, gateway-specific outages exceeded shared ones, yet sensors kept delivering through other gateways (one edge sensor excepted).
- Risk to manage: the reviewers' likely objection is "an evaluation of known tools". The answer must be in the framing (what was not known before this study) and in the limits analysis (why simple rules suffice), not in new acronyms.
